# Phase D — grading against the rules, 14 August 2026

[← Documentation index](README.md) · [the plan that scoped it](engine-source-plan-2026-08-12.md) · [the discarded night that designed it](night-plan-2026-08-13.md)

**Status: D0, D1 and D2 shipped. D3 closed without running it, and the reason is
below.** `utils/search_oracle.py` and `utils/tie_census.py`, both halves passing,
raw runs in `log/dia-2026-08-14/`.

**Why it exists.** Every other instrument in this project grades the agent
against another heuristic, and the reference bot is **saturated** — we win 95 %
of simulated games, so anything that decides a close game is measured in a
vanishing fraction of them. That caveat withheld both of the day's list
candidates and it ends every report on this page's date. A rollout to game end
grades against the engine's own rules instead.

⚠️ **The oracle reads the opponent's hand and can never be a play-time policy.**
It is a grader for games we already hold both sides of. The module says so in its
first paragraph and a test asserts it.

---

## 1. It works, and it is cheap

    9.3 ms per full rollout · 0.06 ms/step · 143 steps to game end

Cheaper than the plan's own S4 estimate of 0.02 s. Two options at K=100 cost
**1.9 s per decision**, and the whole 279-tie census runs in about five minutes.

---

## 2. Four things it measured about itself, each of which edits the plan

**The prizes are unknowable, even to their owner.** The plan defaulted to an
*omniscient* determinization — "read the true hidden state from the battle we are
driving". Half of it cannot be read: a seat's own prize cards come back `None` in
that seat's own observation. So the determinization is part omniscient (their
hand, which their view does show) and part **sampled**, and K averages over the
sampling.

**`search_begin` accepts a determinization that does not add up.** It validates
only `len(...) < count`: too few cards is an error, **too many is silently
tolerated**. The first version here handed the opponent 46 cards for a deck of
40 — it counted hand, bench, active and discard and forgot that an attached
energy, a tool and a pre-evolution are cards too — and the engine played the game
without a word. `determinize` now closes its own arithmetic per seat and raises.

**The API is not seeded**, so the oracle is an **estimator**, not a replay. Same
seed, same choice, same determinization: 140 steps against 122.

**And its noise floor is not one number.** The first floor committed here was a
single pair of batches (17 pp / 0.03) and the next run of the same test returned
7 pp / 0.33 — the instrument catching its own author in exactly the error it
exists to prevent. Measured over six pairs:

| K | winrate floor, median (worst) | margin floor, median (worst) |
|---:|---|---|
| 20 | 7.5 pp (**30.0**) | 0.28 (0.70) |
| 50 | 6.0 pp (8.0) | 0.10 (0.36) |
| 100 | 4.0 pp (6.0) | 0.09 (0.24) |

**K=20 — the plan's own number — is not enough for anything.**

## 3. The determinizer took three attempts, and the guard caught all three

Worth writing down because every one of them would have produced a *plausible*
wrong grade, and the only reason none did is that `determinize` refuses to close.

| # | the defect | how it showed |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | attached energies, tools and pre-evolutions were not counted as cards | 46 cards for a deck of 40 — the engine accepted it |
| 2 | the opponent's whole board was read from **their last observation**, which is stale by the time we decide | two cards short on 3 of 15 boards |
| 3 | their hand was **subtracted** from `seen` after `seen` moved to the current observation, where their hand was never included | four cards short, and it deleted real board cards that merely shared an id |

Two guesses were wrong before the third was measured — `looking` and the
in-flight effect card were added on a hypothesis and changed nothing. Both are
kept: they are cards in no zone and the accounting is right to include them.

The rule that came out of it: **`seen` always comes from the current
observation** (their board is public and is right there), their view is used
**only** for the hand, and only when its length matches the current `handCount`.
Otherwise the hand is sampled like everything else. Eight tests pin it.

## 4. D2 — where the scorer has no opinion, the rules mostly have none either

A tie is **the top two options in the same tier with scores within ε**. A
different tier is not a tie: the order already settled it.

The census reproduces the population that was already on record by another
route — **279 exact ties over 2 035 MAIN menus** of the frozen corpus, against
the 280 the tier work counted.

Graded at K=50, and run **twice** with the corrected determinizer:

| class | n | run 1 | run 2 | verdict |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| ATTACH ~ ATTACH | 94 | −0.003 [−0.080, +0.073] | −0.008 [−0.093, +0.077] | **indifference** |
| PLAY ~ PLAY | 85 | +0.072 [−0.016, +0.160] | −0.031 [−0.117, +0.055] | **indifference** |
| ABILITY ~ ABILITY | 61 | +0.018 [−0.091, +0.127] | −0.014 [−0.144, +0.117] | **indifference** |
| EVOLVE ~ EVOLVE | 28 | −0.006 [−0.158, +0.146] | +0.219 [+0.099, +0.339] | population < 30, **and unstable** |
| **ABILITY ~ ATTACH** | 11 | **+0.631 [+0.353, +0.909]** | **+0.755 [+0.556, +0.953]** | population < 30, **but stable** |

**Two hundred and forty ties where the agent's own scorer says "these are worth
the same" and the rules, rolled to the end, agree.** That is the result, and for
a scorer this heavily tuned it is the likely one: the ties are ties. Stop paying
attention to them.

⚠️ **Running it twice is what makes this table readable, and it should be the
habit.** `EVOLVE ~ EVOLVE` moved from −0.006 to +0.219 between two runs of the
same census on the same boards — further than either interval allows. The
per-class interval is computed from between-*tie* variance and does not carry the
between-*run* variance of resampling the hidden state. **A class is not settled
until two independent runs agree**, and on this table exactly one of the five
does.

### The one class with an opinion is the one with a history

All eleven `ABILITY ~ ATTACH` ties are the same decision: **attach a Basic Grass
by hand, or use Hydrapple ex's Ripening Charge** — the exact axis where making
`ABILITY:150` the owner was measured, came out neutral, and was **reverted**
([[el-adjunte-del-turno-y-la-habilidad-empatan-sobre-el-mismo-cuerpo]]).

The oracle says the agent's current choice — the manual attachment — is worth
**+0.63 to +0.76 prizes**, positive in 10 of 11, across two corpus runs. And a
third, independent population agrees: **15 ties harvested from 200 self-play
games on today's list, graded against the opponent's REAL hand, +0.647 [+0.352,
+0.941]**.

**Three populations, two lists, two determinization qualities, same answer.** The
revert was right, and there is now a positive reason for it rather than a neutral
gate plus a policy. It stays *not concluded* by the written criterion — 11 and 15
are both under 30 — and that is the honest label, but it is the strongest signal
this instrument has produced.

## 5. D3 — closed without spending a rollout on it

The plan's D3 was *settle `op_wins_after_ko` with rollouts*. It is already
settled, and more conclusively than a rollout could: its shelf is **0.02 % of
decisions against real opponent decks**, 94 % of that shelf is boards already
lost, and the flag predicts at 54 %
([[op-wins-after-ko-es-una-moneda-al-aire-el-94-por-ciento-de-su-estante-ya-esta-perdido]]).
Pointing the oracle at it would spend it on a population below its own noise
floor, which is exactly what this project's discipline forbids. **Recorded as
closed by frequency, not measured again.**
