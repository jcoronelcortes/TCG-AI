## 1. The criterion, written before anything runs

A variant is **recommended** only if, against the current sixty, measured paired
over the same 133 lists with the same seeds:

1. the **weighted prize delta is positive**, and
2. the **winrate delta's 95 % interval excludes zero**.

Anything else is reported as *measured, not recommended*. An ambiguous result
counts as NOT passed ([[una-clausula-de-criterio-puede-ser-imposible-de-cumplir]]).

**Nothing is merged into `deck.csv`.** The list is the deck owner's decision
(the night's Q1), and a day of unattended measurement does not get to change the
sixty cards that are on the ladder right now.

### The evidence a variant has to come from

Not taste. Each swap below names the measurement that suggested it:

* the **card census on the new sixty** (10 635 games): the worst converters are
  Xerosic's Machinations 18.7 %, Unfair Stamp 21.8 %, Boss's Orders 29.2 %,
  Dawn 31.4 %;
* the **hard matchups lose by starvation, not misplay**
  ([[las-derrotas-de-los-matchups-duros-son-hambre-no-mala-jugada]]), which
  argues for fuel and engine over tech;
* **S1**: with one Tapu Bulu the Cornerstone whitelist reaches a single card —
  it still fires (4 of 251) and wins every time, but the question of the second
  copy is now a measurable one.

### ⚠️ The family that simulation may not decide

**Unfair Stamp, Fezandipiti ex and the recovery package (Night Stretcher,
Lana's Aid) cannot be judged on simulated numbers**
([[el-bot-generico-tasa-como-muertas-las-cartas-de-remontada]]): they only do
their work from behind, and against the reference bot we are almost never
behind — Unfair Stamp converts 23.5 % simulated against 72.1 % on the real
ladder. Variants touching them are **measured and reported, never recommended**,
and the report has to say which of the two categories each result falls in.

## 2. Wave 1 — four single-card swaps

Every variant keeps 60 cards and respects the four-copy rule. Each is measured
against **A2** (`log/noche-2026-08-14/L-A2.txt`, the current sixty, same code,
same corpus, same 400 seeds), so the comparison is paired and free.

| # | swap | where it comes from | may it be recommended? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **V1** | −1 Xerosic's Machinations → +1 Basic {G} Energy | the list's worst converter, traded for fuel against the starvation matchups | yes |
| **V2** | −1 Boss's Orders → +1 Basic {G} Energy | same axis, second worst multi-copy | yes |
| **V3** | −1 Dawn → +1 Poké Pad | the second Poké Pad converted exactly like the first (68.3 %); does a third? Dawn converts 31.4 % | yes |
| **V4** | −1 Dawn → +1 Tapu Bulu | the direct test of S1: does the second Tapu Bulu earn its slot back? | yes |

**What the wave cannot tell us**, and it is stated now rather than discovered
later: these are *marginal* swaps measured one at a time. A pair of them may
interact — cutting both Xerosic and Boss's is not V1 plus V2 — and no
combination is measured until a single swap has earned it.

---

## 3. What happens after wave 1

Wave 2 is chosen by wave 1's results, and by that rule alone:

* if a swap passes, the next wave tests **its second copy** (does the axis
  continue?) and its **combination** with any other passing swap;
* if none passes, the next wave stops trading cards and tests **quantities on
  the axis the night already validated** — the fourteenth Grass was the only
  card that diluted, so a fifteenth is a hypothesis with a prior;
* if the whole search comes back flat, that is a result: the sixty are at a
  local optimum for this instrument, and the honest next step is the one the
  night named — **phase D, a grader against the rules** rather than more
  measurement against a bot we beat 95 % of the time.
