## 1. Track G — the seven red tests, and not one of them was a regression

All seven traced to the same cause, and it is worth stating in one line because
it will happen again on the next list change:

> **A replay is a game of the list of its day.** The agent seeds its whole deck
> belief from `deck.csv` (`main.py:165`), so replaying a recorded board against
> a later list files every copy the new list holds and the old one did not as a
> **prize**. The belief then places seven cards in six, the in-flight arbiter
> fires on an impossible state it was never meant to see, and decisions move
> with no rule having changed.

| # | Test | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| G1 | `test_ld_committed_supporter` ×2 | **Stale record.** The record's Last-Ditch reveals all 41 remaining cards with no Meowth ex among them, so the second copy is *provably* prized — and only the recorded list reaches that belief. Against today's sixty the arbiter books the Meowth to the discard, `do_not_shuffle_the_last_xerosic` stops firing, and the module's control half measures the gap between two decks |
| G2 | `test_real_opponents_mirror` | **Derived column, stale.** `solape_propio` is a function of `deck.csv` |
| G3 | `test_the_card_census_closes_on_sixty` | **Stale record.** 62 fates for a 60-card deck |
| G4 | `test_the_frozen_corpus_runs_on_a_clean_checkout` | **Stale record.** One flip, `registro_035` turn 6, on nothing but the extra Grass |
| G5 | `test_the_ultra_ball_in_flight_becomes_a_prize` ×2 | **Stale record.** Belief of 8 prizes with 6 face down |

### ⚠️ The plan's own recommendation was wrong, and the tree says so

Q2(a) — the recommended default, and the one that was answered — says to
**re-freeze the corpus after reading its flip**. Reading it is what showed that
re-freezing would have been a mistake: the flip is not a decision the agent
changed, it is the agent believing a deck nobody played. `freeze_corpus.py
--snapshot-only` would have written that wrong belief into the golden snapshot
**for good**, and every future flip would have been measured against it.

What was done instead: `golden_corpus.replay_data` replays every record under
**the list of the record**. The flip disappears with no snapshot rewritten, and
the corpus goes back to answering the only question it exists for — did a *rule*
change. The same seam is where a record harvested after a future list change
will have to carry its own list.

### What shipped for it

* `tests/recorded_deck.py` — `deck_of_record()`, which swaps `my_deck` and the
  belief seeded from it, plus `tests/fixtures/deck_2026-08-13.csv`, the list
  every recording on disk was played with.
* `utils/card_census.py --our-deck` — same reason, for the census.
* `utils/real_opponents.py --refresh-overlap` — recomputes `solape_propio`
  in place, without replaying the screening games or re-deciding admissions that
  were measured on another day. **What to run the day `deck.csv` changes.**

**Result: 2572 passed, 23 skipped. `lint_architecture`: no violations.** Three
commits on `main`, nothing pushed.

### And a fact for the playbook

**Nobody in the corpus plays our exact list any more.** The 60/60 mirror is now
**58/60**. It is still a mirror by the 40/60 threshold, so the caveat class
survives — but the playbook's sentence "someone in the top-500 plays our exact
list" is false as of today.

Against the *recorded* list, +1 Poké Pad and +1 Basic Grass is exactly the new

**The ladder cannot judge this change and will not be able to for a long time.**

reading noise. What the simulated arms below can say, they say with 53 181
games per arm.

Two consequences that are not about the number:

* the census refuses to pool two lists into one table, and it was right to —
  the 196-game pooled view above mixes them and is quoted here only to show
  the alarm;
* **P1 (Deluxe Bomb) is blocked tonight.** Its fixture needs episode 92355371
  and the ladder API serves only recent replays: 90 came down, that one is not
  among them. It goes back to the pending list with the reason attached rather

## 6. P3 — the Festival Lead seat gap belongs to the mirrors, not to Festival Lead

The playbook left P5 open: *investigate why opening costs us against Festival
Lead (−1.1 pp at 11 % of the top-100), confounded by the mirror class.* The
confound is the whole of it.

| | A1 (old list) | A2 (new list) |
|---|---|---|
| Festival Lead, all 15 lists | −0.63 pp ±1.10 | −0.71 pp ±0.98 |
| Festival Lead, **without the mirrors** | +0.82 pp ±1.37 | −0.04 pp ±1.30 |
| **the mirrors alone** | **−1.78 pp ±1.64** ⭐ | −1.24 pp ±1.41 |
| the whole meta | +2.08 pp ±0.37 | +1.04 pp ±0.37 |

**Going first is genuinely worse against copies of our own list** — the only row
whose interval excludes zero — and the mirrors are 7 of the 15 Festival Lead
lists, more than half of that archetype by weight. Against real Festival Lead
lists the seat is indistinguishable from zero in both arms. **P5 closes with a
measurement and no rule**: there was nothing to fix.

And an unasked-for finding beside it: **going first is worth half of what it was
under the new list** — +2.08 pp → +1.04 pp, two intervals that do not overlap.
The new sixty is less seat-dependent, which is the shape a list with more energy
and a second Poké Pad would have if it opened more reliably.

## 7. Track C — what the four cards actually do, card by card

10 635 seeded games against the 133 lists by meta share, crossed against the
13 August run on the old list (`card_census.py --compare`). Spearman on
conversion **+0.968**: the swap did not reorder the list, it moved four rows.

| card | old list | new list | |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **Tapu Bulu** 2 → 1 | 43.7 % | **53.3 %** | +9.6, up five places |
| **Night Stretcher** 2 → 1 | 40.7 % | **46.2 %** | +5.5, up two |
| **Poké Pad** 1 → 2 | 68.3 % | **68.3 %** | +0.1 — the second copy converts exactly like the first |
| **Basic {G} Energy** 13 → 14 | 85.8 % | 83.7 % | −2.1, the only dilution |

**This is what a good swap looks like from inside.** The two cards that lost a
copy are *used more often*, which is what a 1-of does when the 2-of was
redundant; the added Poké Pad carries its own weight rather than sitting in
hand; and the only card that converts worse is the fourteenth Grass, which is
the expected price of adding a copy of something already abundant — and the
cheapest possible one, since an unspent Grass is not a spent slot.

The two worst converters of the list are unchanged and still the same two:
**Xerosic's Machinations 18.7 %** and **Unfair Stamp 21.8 %**. The card census's
own caution stands: neither can be judged on simulated numbers
([[el-bot-generico-tasa-como-muertas-las-cartas-de-remontada]]), and §4's real
games are too few to judge them either.

## 8. S1 — the Cornerstone whitelist narrowed, and it is not dead

The list change left `_ns_crustle_allowed_basics` reaching **one card**: it is
`(Tapu_Bulu, Pinsir)`, Pinsir is not in the list, and Tapu Bulu is now a 1-of.
The commit that adapted its test said the case "no longer separates the whitelist
from the generic scorer", which is true of the *test* and says nothing about the
rule's traffic. So it was measured: `rule_census.py`, 120 games against two
Cornerstone decks, both halves of the self-test passing.

* `_ESC_NS_CRUSTLE[0] basico_whitelist`: chain resolved **251**, evaluated 251,
  **fired 4, decided 4, won 4**. **The rule is not inert** — it is rare (1.6 %,
  above the project's 0.5 % floor) and it wins every time it fires, which is
  exactly the shape predicted: with a single Tapu Bulu it can only fire when
  that copy is in the discard, and when it is, recovering it is right.
* Its three evolution siblings — `dipplin_con_applin`, `bayleef_con_chikorita`,
  `meganium_con_bayleef` — are `EVALUADA, NUNCA DISPARA` over 177 chain
  resolutions. **That is by construction and not a finding:**
  `_ns_crustle_evos_permitidas` returns `()` against pure Cornerstone, and it
  returned `()` before the list changed too. Nobody should "fix" them on the
  strength of this zero.

And the matchup agrees: `otro_cornerstone_mask_ogerpon_ex_1` is **+1.10 pp** in
A2 − A1. The list change did not cost the Cornerstone matchup; what cost it a
little was the day's code (−0.50 pp in A1 − A0), which is the row to keep an eye
on rather than the whitelist.

**S3, checked and empty:** no scoring constant in `main.py` or `ptcg/` was
calibrated against the old counts. The only list-dependent numbers are the
fishing probabilities (which follow `deck.csv` correctly and were re-pinned in
the list commit), `_has_recovery` (correct, just satisfied less often now), and

## 10. Judging the premise

The plan said it would be **falsified** if both deltas landed inside their
intervals — if thirteen commits and four cards were jointly worth nothing the
meta could see — or if all seven red tests were merely stale.

Neither happened. Both deltas exclude zero, and the red tests were stale *and*
carried a real defect in the harness that would have corrupted the golden corpus
had the plan's own recommendation been followed without reading first. The night
that was designed to re-establish ground found that the ground had moved
**twice**, measured each move separately, and left the second one — Mega Lopunny
— for a human to read.
