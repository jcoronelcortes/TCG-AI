## The headline: three defects nobody had read

All three were found by counting rather than by reading, which is the whole
argument of the plan.

### 1. A rule that is asked 61 619 times and never once fires

`_RULES_MEOWTH_FETCH[10] xerosic_alakazam` is **dominated**, not rare. Its
predicate is exactly `[9] xerosic_priority_over_boss` plus `win_via_boss`, and
the arithmetic proves it: rule 9 is evaluated 62 072 times and fires 453; rule 10
is evaluated 61 619 — the difference, every single time — and fires **zero**. The
newer rule swallowed the older one's ladder, so the 1260/1200 split by
`strong_attacker` is never applied and everything lands on 9's flat 1310.

Alongside it: three separate `supporter_already_played` vetoes (LILLIE, BOSS and
XEROSIC play chains) asked 11 850–13 417 times each and firing never. In the PLAY
context that flag is never True when a Supporter is on the menu, exactly as in
the DISCARD context it is never False. One flag, two callers, degenerate in both
— the `93a27eb` family.

Instrument: `utils/rule_census.py` (`09b5432`).

### 2. The latch fires, and sixty lines later a general rule undoes it

`Lillie's Determination` is latched in the discard ladder — one copy protected at
2, the spare released at 72 — and then a card-agnostic block runs
`score = min(score, DISCARD_SUPPORTER_LIVE_KEEP)`, which is 2, for the Supporter
with the highest live value whenever another Supporter shares the hand. "The best
Supporter I could still play" is exactly as true of the second copy as of the
first, so the spare is re-protected and **the latch never reaches the menu**.

That is the general rule overriding its own special case, and it happens in four
different records — four times as often as the Meowth ex flip Wave 1 left
standing, which the same audit reproduces independently.

    12 duplicate pairs share a keep-band score across 118 discard menus
    Lillie's x4 · Meowth ex · Teal Mask Ogerpon ex · Meganium · Unfair Stamp

Instrument: `utils/duplicate_protection_audit.py` (`5716f30`).

### 3. Twelve menus of a hundred and eighteen pay with the fuel

The pending question — the cost of a search discarding the energy that fuels the
body it is buying — now has its number:

    12 of 118 discard menus (10.2%) drop a Basic Grass ahead of an evolution the
    agent itself calls ORPHANED (pre-evolution neither in play nor in hand)
    24 card-to-card pairs · Meganium x5, Hydrapple ex x5, Bayleef, Dipplin
    nine of the twelve are the cost of a search of OURS, three are forced
    five of them are the LAST energy in hand

Two orders of magnitude above the 0.11% of the cost/search rule, so this one
**can** be put to self-play — unlike almost everything else touched this week.
It is a policy change over every forced discard and it is not made here.

Instrument: `utils/fodder_ladder_audit.py` (`13ff377`).

## The linter learned the day's bugs

Three rules, one per defect that shipped on 10 August, none of which showed up as
a red test (`b9b25e9`):

* **R6** — a test that READS a `records/` file must carry a skip guard.
* **R7** — a `utils/gate_*.py` loading two arms must define AND call
  `provenance()`.
* **R8** — inside the DISCARD block the turn flags may only be read to build the
  horizon.

**R7 was not clean when it was written, which is the point of it.**
`utils/gate_promoted_relay.py` loads two arms, patches two seams by two different
routes — an attribute on the `retreat` module and an entry in the globals dict of
the module defining `_bench_finisher_upgrade` — and never asked whether either
patch had landed. `from ... import` binds a copy, so the attribute route reaches
only callers that go through the module object: rename either seam and the
neutralisation lands on nothing while the run prints a winrate. It now has a
`provenance()` that needs no board and refuses to measure when the arms agree.

## Two detectors caught their own authors

Worth recording separately, because it is the pattern the night was built on.

**The rule census failed its own self-test on the first run, and was right to.**
The specificity half reported a rule as having "decided" while the bands called
it dead. Both read the same counter and only one was right: in an ARGMAX chain
every rule that fires calls `value`, so a `value` call there is participation,
not decision. `Contadores.mando()` is the repair.

**Two audits disagreed about how many menus the corpus has.** The capture keyed
menus by `id(tc.select)` without holding a reference, and CPython reuses an
address the moment the object at it is collected: distinct menus landed on the
same key and merged. The same corpus reported 90, then 98. It is 118, and every
rate published before the fix had the wrong denominator. The value now holds the
select; three consecutive runs agree.

The general lesson, written into memory: **run a deterministic instrument two or
three times before believing a number it gives you.**

## The one behaviour change, measured and parked

The cap outside its matchup (`XEROSIC_BIG_HAND`, branch
`xerosic-cap-reads-their-hand`, commit `a2c3ff3`) is complete — rule, gate,
census, seven tests, reviewed flip — and deliberately **not** on the working
branch.

    census: 1 of 3 580 decisions (0.03%), below the 0.5% bar the queued note set
    for itself, so no games were played and none should be

The single flip is correct (it drops a gust `_supp_values` had already priced at
zero and keeps a cap the play scorer prices at 3380 — the same self-contradiction
`5040fa9` removed from the other half of the Ultra Ball). But the suite says what
the census does not: it breaks **four assertions belonging to two rules that
landed the same day**, whose controls pin the literal 60 or use the Xerosic as
their example of a card the ladder eats.

Rewriting this week's controls to accommodate a 0.03% change, overnight, with
nobody awake to say whether those controls meant the constant or the intent, is
the quiet erosion this repository keeps naming. **The decision left for the
morning is exactly that question**, and the branch is ready either way.
