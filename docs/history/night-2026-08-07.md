## 3. When we take the knockout, the plan stops looking

`build_turn_plan` zeroes its whole defensive half wherever our own attack knocks
their active out. True of the body in front -- it is on its way to the discard --
and silent about the board, because a knockout forces a **promotion**, and unlike
a bench swap or a gust the bench they promote from is fully visible.

Shipped as **data only** (`op_prizes_after_ko`, `op_wins_after_ko`), reading the
worst of their benched bodies through the same projector, on a snapshot with
their bench correctly one body smaller.

**No rule was built on it, and the census is why.** Nested populations over 2,485
mirror decisions: we take the knockout 27.3% → the promoted body replies 12.1% →
that closes their count 2.9% → a retreat is on the menu 1.9% → **a relay of ours
takes the same knockout and survives what comes up: 0.08%** -- two boards, and on
one of them the agent already retreats. Four real archetypes agree (Ogerpon Verde
0.31%, Alakazam 0.08%, Crustle 0.00%, Marnie 0.00%).

## 4. The five principles of tournament play

Full audit in [tournament-principles-audit.md](tournament-principles-audit.md). Summary:

| Principle | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Prize mapping | Implemented **for one turn** (`TurnPlan`). No multi-turn route: nothing asks whether the remaining prizes are still reachable on their board. |
| 2. Deck checking / prizes | **Implemented, and better than the principle asks.** Prizes are re-derived on every full deck reveal, per-zone census, draw odds, and searches are downgraded when their target is prized. |
| 3. Sequencing | **Implemented and dense.** Supporter before retreat, attack last, search before the item lock, gust waits for the charge. |
| 4. Resource management | Implemented **case by case** rather than as a reading. No single "how many prizes are sitting on our bench that a gust can reach". |
| 5. Reading the opponent | **Half.** Archetype from their discard, two or three turns early. Their TEMPO is not read at all: `_op_disruption_belief` takes an `op_supporter_played` parameter and ignores it, and nothing tracks their discard between turns. |

The two real holes are the multi-turn prize route and the opponent's tempo. Both
are computable from data already in the observation. **Neither is measurable with
this harness** -- the generic bot has no "stuck hand" state to read -- which is
why neither became a rule tonight.

## 5. Where the worst matchup actually loses

`crustle_wall_6` is a pure stall build: 4 Crustle, 4 Jumbo Ice Cream, 4 Cook, 3
Waitress, 13 Grass, no Boss's Orders at all. Autopsy over 120 games: 46 losses,
**37 of them on prizes**, and **174 sterile turns across 45 lost games**.

Two findings came out of it.

**a) 83% of our damage is healed back.** `utils/healing_census.py` follows every
opposing body by serial: against `crustle_wall_6` we deal 12,720 damage and 10,600
of it is put back before it becomes a prize. Against `crustle_wall_2`, 44%; against
Marnie, 26%; in the mirror, 0% (the method's noise floor). **55 of the 97 real
lists carry healing and the agent reads none of it.** No rule was written: the
projections are never *wrong* -- the observation's HP is always current -- and the
decision that healing should change has not been isolated yet.

**b) The dry turns all have the same shape, and one of them was a lost prize.**
`utils/wall_probe.py` dumped 22 turns that ended dry behind the wall. Every one
has our active at **zero energy with no RETREAT in the menu**, and several have a
fully charged Tapu Bulu rotting on the bench -- `seco_013` through `seco_017` are
five consecutive turns of it, at 5 prizes to 1, with two charged Tapu on the bench
and a 150 HP Crustle in front.

`seco_019` is the one that is a plain miss, and it became section 6.

## 6. The lethal line that was outbid by its own special case

Turn 14: active **Meowth ex at 0 energy** (retreat cost 1, so no RETREAT in the
menu), one Grass in hand, **Tapu Bulu with four Grass** on the bench, **Crustle
170/170** in front. Grass → active, retreat, promote, Wood Hammer 220: a prize.
The agent attached to a benched Dipplin at zero energy and ended the turn.

The detector was never the problem: `_grass_unlocks_active_retreat` returns
(True, False) on this board and `_attach_enable_retreat_ko` documents its own band
as **41000**, "a LETHAL line for this turn". It never got there. The `elif` chain
tested `_tapu_sac_enable_retreat` first -- the older, narrower version of the same
idea, whose subject is *a Tapu Bulu with four Grass*, which is the entire plan of
the Crustle matchup -- and that branch scores **24000**. A routine bench charge
scores 31000.

Fixed by putting the general rule ahead of its own special case.

**Measured, and it is NEUTRAL.** Two arms of 3,000 games per list, swapping the
one file on disk because `--base` only swaps `main.py`:

| List | candidate | base |
| --- | --- | --- |
| crustle_wall_6 | 56.5% [54.7–58.2] | 56.5% [54.8–58.3] |
| crustle_wall_2 | 75.2% [73.7–76.7] | 74.7% [73.1–76.3] |
| crustle_wall_9 | 83.6% [82.3–84.9] | 84.5% [83.2–85.8] |
| marnie_grimmsnarl_1 | 95.9% [95.1–96.6] | 96.5% [95.8–97.1] |
| alakazam_1 | 95.0% [94.2–95.7] | 95.1% [94.3–95.8] |

The two that leaned negative were replicated and **both flipped sign**:
crustle_wall_9 came back 83.9% against 82.9%, and Marnie -- 38% of the meta, so
the one that had to be settled -- came back at n=6000 per arm at 96.2% against
96.1%. Three readings of Marnie: −0.6, −0.7, +0.1.

Golden corpus 0 flips. The frequency explains it: the stuck active one Grass from
its retreat cost happens on **6.3%** of decisions against `crustle_wall_6`, but
with a bench knockout available on 0.07%, and the shadowed Tapu subset on 0.06%
(0.28% against `crustle_wall_2`).

Kept under the project's own exception -- neutral gets reverted *unless it
corrects a value that was demonstrably wrong*. A rule receiving 24000 where its
documentation specifies 41000, and losing to a charge it exists to outrank, is a
wrong value and not a preference.

## 7b. Ogerpon Verde: the census landed, the rule did not

The matchup where **our own energy is their damage** -- Myriad Leaf Shower reads
`30 + 30 x (their active's energy + OURS)` and `ogerpon_verde_1` is four Teal
Mask Ogerpon ex and eighteen Grass. Measured over 4,723 boards with their Ogerpon
active:

```
their Myriad, mean                                 172.2
... of which energy on OUR active                   46.9   (27.3%)
boards where their attack already kills our active  58.6%
... and would NOT without our own energy            15.1%
```

Against `ogerpon_verde_10`: 44.9% of their damage is ours, and 17.3% of the
boards are lethal only because of it. There is no matchup flag for this deck at
all (there is one for Crustle, for Cornerstone, for Kangaskhan, for Starmie).

A rule was written -- degrade the attachment onto the active when it is the one
that flips their projected blow from survivable to lethal -- and it **collided
immediately with a measured rule**: `_fee_beats_parking_it` sends the Grass to
the active precisely when the fee unlocks a pivot the turn has not built yet, and
energy spent on a retreat LEAVES the active, so it never reaches their turn. The
two can be made disjoint (require the active to already pay its own retreat
cost), and at that point the rule was no longer the one the census sized.

It was **not shipped**. The census is the deliverable: 15–17% of the boards in a
7.3% archetype is a real target, and it deserves a design that starts from the
collision rather than discovering it.
